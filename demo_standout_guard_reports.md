
## Guard 1

**Guard name:** Officer Lena Morris

**Account:**

On 05/18/2026 I was posted at the North Tower lobby desk for the 1500 to 2300 shift. At approximately 2148 hours I was finishing the visitor log close out and checking the badge return box when I noticed a male come through the west revolving door with his head down and a insulated delivery cooler in his right hand. I remember the time because I had just wrote 2145 on my log sheet for the pharmacy courier that had left a few minutes before. The male was moving like he already knew where he was going, not wandering around like most visitors do after hours, so I asked him if I could help him and asked to see his badge. He said something like “I’m good, they’re waiting on this upstairs,” and he kept walking toward the elevators.

The subject was a male, I would say late 20s or early 30s, about 5'9 maybe, medium build, short dark hair, no facial hair that I noticed. He had on a navy zip up jacket, black pants, white sneakers, and blue medical gloves on both hands. I did not see a visitor badge on him at any point. He did have a lanyard around his neck but there was no badge card hanging from it, just the empty clip. He was carrying a white and gray cooler bag with a shoulder strap, the kind the lab couriers sometimes use, but it did not have the normal vendor sticker on the side. It looked like one of those soft sided cooler bags with a zipper top.

I stepped from behind the desk and told him again that he needed to stop because the research floors are restricted after 1900 hours. He did not stop until he got to elevator bank B. I pressed the security hold button for the elevators from the lobby panel so the doors would not open for him. He turned around and said “you people always do this” and then tried to scan something at the elevator reader. I could not see what he scanned, it might of been a phone or a card inside his hand, but the reader stayed red. He got agitated and said he had a delivery for “Dr. Keller on seven.” I told him I needed to call the floor before he could go up. He said no because the delivery was temperature sensitive and he started walking fast toward the service hall that leads to the freight elevator.

At that point I radioed Control and asked for roving officer response to the North Tower lobby, unknown male attempting to access seven with a cooler. I followed him but kept about ten feet between us. He pushed through the service hall door before I could get there. The door did alarm because it is not supposed to be used from the lobby side after hours unless opened by badge. I did not touch him and I did not grab him. I want that clear because later he was yelling that I put hands on him, but I did not. I was behind him the whole time and he was moving away from me.

He went down the service hall and tried the freight elevator call button. It did not open. Officer Chen came in from the loading dock end of the hall and told him to put the cooler down and step away from the restricted doors. The subject set the cooler down but not gently, more like dropped it from knee height, and then he backed up with his hands out. I did not see anything spill out of the cooler at that time. The zipper was closed. Officer Chen took control of the cooler and I stayed with the subject and kept him facing away from the freight elevator. He kept saying “call Keller, call Keller, he knows me,” but he would not give me a first name or company name.

Supervisor Brooks arrived a couple minutes later and the subject was escorted back to the lobby. The subject left the property through the west door after Brooks told him he could not remain on site. I did not see him run and I did not see him get into any vehicle because I stayed inside with Control on the radio. I later checked the sign in logs and I could not find any visitor signed in for Dr. Keller after 1800 hours. I also called the seventh floor nurse station and they said they did not have any delivery scheduled, but they are not the research office so I do not know if that is the final answer. To my knowledge nobody got injured and he never made it upstairs. My opinion is he was trying to tailgate or use a old credential, but I cannot say why he was here.

## Guard 2

**Guard name:** Officer Marcus Chen

**Account:**

I was the roving exterior officer assigned to North Tower, South Garage and the loading area on May 18. At about 2157 hrs I was doing a check of the north loading dock doors because we had been having problems with one of the mag locks not catching all the way. While I was at the dock I heard Officer Morris call over the radio about a unknown male in the lobby trying to get to the seventh floor with a cooler bag. I was already close to the service corridor so I entered through the dock side employee door and came up the hall toward the freight elevator area.

When I came around the corner I saw the male subject already inside the service corridor, standing by the freight elevator call station. He was not in the main lobby anymore. He had the cooler bag on the floor beside his right foot and he was kind of bent over the reader like he was trying to scan a badge or maybe punch something into the elevator panel, even though there is no keypad there. I saw a plastic visitor badge clipped to the left side of his jacket, low near the pocket. It was turned around backwards so I could not read the name, but I am pretty sure there was a badge on him. It was not just a empty lanyard from what I saw. The lighting in that corridor is bright so I feel confident about that part, but I did not get close enough to read it before things started moving.

I told the subject, “Security, step back from the elevator and keep your hands where I can see them.” He turned toward me and said he was delivering specimens to Keller lab and that the other guard was making him late. I asked for the cooler to be placed on the floor and for his ID. He said his ID was “with the order” and he put one hand inside his jacket pocket. I told him twice to remove his hand empty. He pulled out a phone, not a weapon, but with the way he was acting and being in a restricted hall I was concerned. Officer Morris was behind him in the hallway. I did see Morris put her hand out and catch his sleeve or elbow when he started to turn back toward the lobby, but it did not look like a grab to me, more like she was trying to keep distance and stop him bumping into her. The subject jerked away and said “don’t touch me.” I did not see her push him or strike him.

There was a moment where the cooler was on the floor and the zipper was partly open. I could see at least two small clear bags inside, and a blue ice pack. I could not read labels and I am not saying they were medical samples for sure, but the cooler was not empty when I saw it. I directed him again to step back, and he did. I moved the cooler behind me with my foot so he could not pick it back up while we waited for Supervisor Brooks. I know that is maybe not ideal evidence handling, but at that moment I was trying to seperate the subject from whatever was in the bag.

The subject did not make it onto the freight elevator and I did not see him enter the seventh floor clean corridor or any research area. He was in the service corridor only from what I observed in person. He kept saying Dr. Keller knew him and that his “old badge should still work,” which made me think maybe he had worked here or delivered here before. He would not provide a company name. Supervisor Brooks arrived at around 2203 and took over. Brooks looked in the cooler while I stood with the subject. I remember Brooks saying there was only an ice pack and packing paper in it, but that is not what I saw earlier, unless the clear bags were just folded packing sleeves or something. I am writing it exactly as I remember it.

After Brooks told him he was trespassed for the night, the subject walked out through the lobby doors. I followed to the exterior doors and watched him turn left on Harbor Ave. I did not see a car pick him up. He was upset and using profanity but he was not physically fighting us. I completed a radio log and checked the freight elevator after. It was functioning normal and there was no damage to the reader or door. I recommend checking access control records for any failed badge reads at elevator B and the freight elevator between 2145 and 2205 because that will show what credential or device he was trying to use.

## Guard 3

**Guard name:** Security Supervisor Dana Brooks

**Account:**

I was the shift supervisor for the swing shift on Monday 05/18/26. I was in the security office reviewing camera bookmarks when Control advised me there was a possible unauthorized access attempt at North Tower, involving a male subject with a cooler or medical bag. I pulled up the North Tower lobby cameras and the service corridor camera before responding. Based on the camera time stamp the first time I can see the subject is 2142:31 when he enters through the west revolving door. He stood just inside the door for maybe twenty seconds and looked toward the desk, then continued across the lobby. So I am not sure about the 2148 or 2157 times listed in the radio traffic. Those may be when Morris first challenged him or when Chen arrived, but camera shows him entering earlier at 2142.

On camera the subject appears to be wearing a dark blue jacket and black or very dark gray pants. I cannot confirm his exact age or race from the camera angle. I also cannot confirm if he had a real visitor badge. There is something light colored clipped around the left side of his jacket or hanging off a lanyard, but it swings around and the camera quality is not good enough for me to say if it was an active badge, expired visitor sticker, or just a plastic holder. From the desk camera I can see Officer Morris step out from the desk area and speak to him near elevator bank B. The subject is seen trying the elevator reader one time. The reader light is red. He then changes direction and goes through the service hall door toward the freight elevator. The service hall alarm is visible on the access control panel at 2144:08.

The part that may matter is the service corridor camera. It shows the subject move past the freight elevator alcove and put his left hand on the clean corridor door handle for the seventh floor prep route. That door is not supposed to be accessed from that side by visitors. He did not get fully through the clean corridor door, but the door appears to open approximately six inches before it shuts again. I cannot tell if he opened it with a badge, if it did not latch, or if the camera angle is making it look wider than it was. I am saying he did make contact with that door and it may have opened partly. Officer Chen arrived from the dock side about ten seconds after that. This is different then saying he was only at the freight elevator. From the video he was at the clean corridor door first, then moved back toward the freight elevator alcove when Chen approached.

I arrived in person at approximately 2201 hours. When I got there, Morris was near the lobby end of the service corridor, Chen was between the subject and the freight elevator, and the subject was standing with his hands visible. The subject was upset but not actively combative when I arrived. He stated his name was “Evan” but refused to provide a last name. He said he had a delivery for “Keller lab” and that he used to come through “all the time before the vendor change.” He also said one of the guards “grabbed him first.” I reviewed the hallway camera after and I do see Morris put a hand toward his arm when he turns, but from the video I cannot determine if there was actual contact. There is no punch, shove, takedown, or anything like that visible. The subject pulled away and pointed at her, so there may have been light contact.

I inspected the cooler bag after Chen moved it away from the subject. When I opened it, I saw one blue ice pack, crumpled brown packing paper, and two empty clear plastic specimen sleeves. The sleeves did not have vials or tubes inside them when I looked. There were no labels with patient information visible to me and no liquid or spill in the bag. Chen told me he thought he saw small clear bags inside earlier, which matches the sleeves I found, but I did not find any actual samples. I did not photograph the inside of the cooler, and in hindsight I probably should have before handling it. The subject was allowed to take the cooler with him after I confirmed there was no company property or identifiable medical material inside.

I informed the subject he was not authorized to be on property after hours, and that if he had a legitimate delivery his company needed to schedule through receiving during approved hours or have the lab contact Security directly. He left the building at approximately 2208 hours through the west lobby doors. Exterior camera 3 shows him walking west on Harbor Ave and getting into a dark compact sedan parked near the bus stop at 2210 hours. I could not read the plate due to glare from the street light. No injuries were reported. I placed a temporary alert on the lobby post orders for any future Keller lab deliveries and emailed Facilities about the clean corridor door to verify it latched correctly. The reason for the subject’s attempted access is still unknown to me.
